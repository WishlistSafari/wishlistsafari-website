Where to see a pangolin on safari

The pangolin is the holy grail of safari sightings: a scaly, ant-eating mammal that many lifelong guides have never seen. It is also the most trafficked mammal in the world, so we only ever describe broad regions.

Quick answer

The southern Kalahari in South Africa is the best-known region for pangolin sightings, at private reserves that run long-term research programmes. The best time is the cooler months, roughly May to August, when pangolins often forage in daylight. Even there, a sighting is never guaranteed.

Occasional sightings across Greater Kruger, Madikwe and northern Botswana, almost always a lucky surprise.

Tips from the field

  • Choose a reserve with an active pangolin research programme.
  • Stay four nights or more, and add aardvark and aardwolf to the same wishlist.
  • Never share exact locations or geotag pangolin photos.

Questions travellers ask

Why are pangolins so rare to see?

They are shy, nocturnal and well camouflaged, and their numbers have been heavily reduced by trafficking.

What does a pangolin eat?

Ants and termites, dug out with strong claws and a long sticky tongue.

Are pangolins dangerous?

No. When threatened, they roll into a tight ball protected by their scales.
