Where to see African wild dogs

African wild dogs, also known as painted wolves, are among the continent's most endangered carnivores, with only a few thousand left in the wild. Watching a pack set off on a hunt is one of the great safari moments.

Quick answer

Northern Botswana, including the Okavango Delta and Moremi, is the stronghold for African wild dogs. Hwange and Mana Pools in Zimbabwe, South Luangwa in Zambia and South Africa's Greater Kruger are also good. The best time is the denning season, roughly June to September, when packs stay close to their pups.

The best places to look

Botswana

Okavango Delta and Moremi

The largest wild dog populations in southern Africa, with packs often seen by camps in private concessions.

Zimbabwe

Hwange and Mana Pools

Mana Pools is famous for walking safaris where you may see a pack on foot. Hwange holds healthy numbers too.

Zambia

South Luangwa

Packs are regularly seen, especially early in the dry season.

South Africa

Greater Kruger and Madikwe

Kruger's packs range widely; malaria-free Madikwe is a good family option.

Tips from the field

  • Time your trip for the denning season if wild dogs are top of your list.
  • Ask camps about recent pack movements before you book; they cover vast ranges.
  • Combine two areas to improve your odds.

Questions travellers ask

Why are African wild dogs endangered?

Mainly habitat loss, conflict with people and livestock, and diseases caught from domestic dogs. They need very large areas to survive.

Are African wild dogs and painted wolves the same?

Yes. Painted wolf, painted dog, Cape hunting dog and African wild dog are all names for the same species.

Can you see wild dogs on a walking safari?

Yes, particularly in Mana Pools in Zimbabwe, always with an experienced professional guide.
